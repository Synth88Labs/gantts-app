What's included

Crowdfunding campaigns are won before they launch: the projects that fund fast are the ones that spent weeks building an email list and lining up early backers, so the first day has momentum. This template puts the whole campaign on one timeline, the long pre-launch runway included, in the six stages a campaign runs through:

  • Concept & validation, Define the idea and value proposition, research the market, set your funding goal and budget, design the reward tiers, and choose your platform. Milestone: concept validated.
  • Build campaign assets, Build the prototype or samples, script and film the pitch video, write the campaign page, and design the graphics. Milestone: assets ready.
  • Pre-launch audience, Build a landing page, grow an email list, run community and social, line up press, and secure early-bird commitments worth around 20% of your goal. Milestone: pre-launch list ready.
  • Launch prep, Do a final page review and test, set the launch date and warm up the list, and prepare the launch-day emails and posts. Milestone: ready to launch.
  • Campaign live, Launch, engage backers daily, run a mid-campaign press push, announce stretch goals, and drive the final 48-hour push. Milestone: campaign funded.
  • Close & fulfilment, Thank your backers, send pledge-manager surveys, finalise production, and fulfil and ship the rewards. Milestone: rewards delivered.

Who uses this template

Creators, startups and inventors running a Kickstarter or Indiegogo campaign use this plan to fund a project without launching to no audience, the mistake that stalls campaigns on day one. It covers the whole run from concept and rewards through building a pre-launch email list and the live campaign to production and reward fulfilment, distinct from a nonprofit fundraising drive.

Build your audience before you launch, not after. Campaigns are ranked partly on early momentum, and a project that opens to no email list stalls on day one. Spend the weeks before launch collecting emails and securing early-bird commitments worth around 20% of your goal, so the campaign hits a chunk of its target in the first 48 hours, the single strongest predictor of whether it funds at all.

How to customize it

  1. Set your launch date first and work backwards; a serious pre-launch runway is six to twelve weeks, not a few days.
  2. Keep the email-list and early-bird tasks running for weeks, pre-launch audience-building is the work that actually funds the campaign, not the launch-day post.
  3. Add a row per reward tier under production and fulfilment so a multi-tier campaign shows each one’s manufacturing and shipping.
  4. Treat “secure early-bird commitments” as a milestone tied to a number (≈20% of goal) so you know whether you are ready to launch.
  5. Budget fulfilment realistically, production and shipping run for weeks or months after the campaign closes, and are where under-planned projects lose money.

Scheduling tips

  • The pre-launch list is the campaign. Projects that fund fast almost always launched to a warm email list; the weeks of audience-building before day one matter more than anything you do during the campaign.
  • The first 48 hours decide the outcome. Platforms surface projects with early momentum, so line up enough early-bird backers to hit a visible chunk of your goal on day one rather than hoping for a late surge.
  • The pitch video does the selling. A clear, short video is the single highest-leverage asset; give it real time in the schedule and finish it before launch prep, not during.
  • Plan fulfilment before you launch. Shipping and production costs sink more funded projects than failed ones; price your tiers and schedule fulfilment as seriously as the campaign itself.
  • Keep backers updated during the campaign. Regular updates and replies keep momentum and share-rate up; schedule the engagement rather than going quiet after launch day.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to plan a crowdfunding campaign?

Most successful campaigns spend six to twelve weeks on pre-launch, building an email list, assets and early-bird commitments, before a 30-day live campaign, then weeks or months on fulfilment. The template spreads concept, assets, pre-launch, launch, the live campaign and fulfilment across a timeline so you can see the real runway.

Why is the pre-launch phase so important?

Crowdfunding platforms rank projects partly on early momentum, so a campaign that opens to an existing audience and early-bird commitments hits a chunk of its goal in the first 48 hours, the strongest predictor of funding. The template gives audience-building its own weeks-long phase rather than treating launch day as the start.

Can I show dependencies between campaign tasks?

Yes. Open the template in the free gantts.app editor to link finish-to-start relationships (assets → pre-launch → launch → fulfilment) so if the video slips, launch and everything after shift automatically and your critical path to launch day stays visible.
