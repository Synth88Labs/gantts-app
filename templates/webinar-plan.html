What's included

Webinars fail on the parts either side of the live hour, too little promotion runway, or no follow-up plan. This template puts the whole webinar on one timeline, in the six stages it actually moves through:

  • Plan & topic, Set the goal and audience, choose the topic, format and date, and confirm your speakers and platform. Milestone: webinar confirmed.
  • Registration & assets, Build the registration and landing page, create the slides and content, and design the promotion assets. Milestone: assets ready.
  • Promotion, Announce the webinar and run the email sequence, social and partner promotion, and any paid ads to fill seats. Milestone: registration goal reached.
  • Rehearsal & prep, Prep the speakers and review the content, run a tech check and dry run, and send reminder emails. Milestone: ready to go live.
  • Live event, Go live and host, and moderate the Q&A and engagement. Milestone: webinar delivered.
  • Follow-up, Publish the recording, send follow-up emails, hand leads to sales, and measure the results. Milestone: post-webinar review.

Who uses this template

Marketing, demand-generation and events teams use this plan to run a webinar without promotion runway, speaker prep and follow-up falling through the cracks. It suits B2B marketers and product teams running lead-generation or educational webinars, from topic and registration through promotion and the live event to lead follow-up.

Most of a webinar’s value is created after the live hour. Attendees and no-shows both need a follow-up, the recording, a nurture email, and a hand-off to sales, so plan the follow-up before the event, not in the tired days after it, or the leads you worked to register go cold.

How to customize it

  1. Give promotion at least three to four weeks, most registrations come in the first and last week, but you need the runway between.
  2. Set the registration goal as a milestone and watch it; if you are behind at the halfway point, add promotion rather than hoping.
  3. Run slide creation in parallel with the registration page, they have different owners and no dependency on each other.
  4. Add a row per promotion channel (email, social, partners, paid) so each has its own owner and cadence.
  5. Mark webinar confirmed, assets ready and “ready to go live” as milestones so nothing downstream starts before its inputs exist.

Scheduling tips

  • Promotion runway is the critical path to attendance. Seats fill over weeks, not days; protect the promotion window rather than compressing it to hit a date.
  • Always run a dry run. The tech check and full rehearsal catch the audio, screen-share and hand-off problems that otherwise happen live; schedule it as a hard gate before go-live.
  • Build the follow-up before the event. The recording, nurture emails and sales hand-off should be drafted in advance so they go out while interest is warm.
  • Send reminders, they drive show-up rate. A one-day and one-hour reminder often lifts attendance sharply; put them on the schedule, not in memory.
  • Measure against the goal. Tie the wrap-up back to the registration and pipeline targets from the plan, a webinar that ran but generated no follow-up has not finished.

Frequently asked questions

How far in advance should I plan a webinar?

Most webinars need four to eight weeks, enough runway for promotion and speaker prep. The template spreads planning, registration, promotion, rehearsal, the live event and follow-up across a timeline so you can see whether your date is realistic.

What should a webinar plan include?

At minimum: goal and topic, speakers and platform, a registration page, a promotion sequence, a rehearsal and tech check, the live event, and a follow-up plan. The template gives each its own phase with milestones and owners.

Can I show dependencies between webinar tasks?

Yes. Open the template in the free gantts.app editor to link finish-to-start relationships (confirm → build → promote → rehearse → go live) so downstream work shifts automatically and the critical path stays visible.
